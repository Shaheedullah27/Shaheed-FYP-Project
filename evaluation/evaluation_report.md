## Status and disclosure

- Dataset: **70 synthetic pairs**, 7 domains, 10 pairs per domain.
- The resumes and postings are **synthetic examples written for this evaluation**. They are not scraped vacancies, real applicants, or hiring outcomes.
- Annotation: **single AI annotator (the coding assistant), drafting from the documents under the project author's direction**. Labels were written before the harness was run. No independent human annotator or second reviewer was available, so no inter-annotator agreement is reported and the labels should be treated as AI-drafted until a human audit is done.
- Revision note: after the first harness run, some documents were lengthened and a few out-of-dictionary requirements were added so that length buckets and the dictionary ceiling were covered. No expected matched/missing label was changed.
- Keyword metrics are bounded by the 182-entry dictionary. Human-identified requirements outside it are listed separately and excluded from keyword metrics rather than silently counted as successes or failures.
- TF-IDF similarity is reported descriptively. It is **not** an accuracy percentage and is not converted into one.

Annotation policy, in short: a term is a requirement when the posting asks
the candidate for it; company-context mentions and negations are excluded;
`A or B` is one requirement; repetition never adds a requirement; a resume
mention is evidence only when it is genuine experience. Ambiguous terms are
recorded and excluded rather than guessed.

## Metric definitions

- **Requirement detection (keyword level):** predicted job requirements vs
  annotated requirements. TP = both, FP = predicted only, FN = annotated only.
- **Matched-skill identification:** over the annotated requirement universe,
  TP = annotated matched and predicted matched, FP = predicted matched but not
  annotated matched, FN = annotated matched but not predicted matched, TN =
  annotated missing and not predicted matched.
- **Missing-skill identification:** the complementary unit; predicted missing
  vs annotated missing. Reported separately, not merged into one score.
- **Alternative-group counting:** one unit per annotated `A or B` group. A
  group is correct only when it contributes exactly one requirement and its
  satisfied/missing status matches the annotation.
- **Requirement count:** annotated logical requirements vs predicted
  requirement count after removing annotated ambiguous terms.
- **Negation:** one unit per annotated exclusion. A posting-side exclusion is
  correct when the term is absent from predicted requirements; a resume-side
  negation is correct when the term is absent from predicted matched skills.
- **Micro** metrics sum counts; **macro** metrics average per-pair values.
  Both are reported where meaningful.

## Known limitations

- Seventy synthetic pairs are sufficient for a transparent course-project benchmark, not for claims about the labour market or universal ATS accuracy.
- The labels are single-annotator. No inter-annotator agreement statistic can be reported.
- Labels use canonical dictionary names so that keyword-level comparison is possible; this makes dictionary coverage part of the evaluation boundary.
- Some labels deliberately test edge cases (negation, context traps, alternatives, learning-only mentions) and are not a random sample of postings.
- The dataset is small enough that one pair can move a domain percentage by several points.
- Nine human-identified requirements (for example Storybook, Airflow, Workday, Anaplan) are outside the dictionary. They are recorded per pair as unmeasured_requirements and are excluded from keyword metrics; the report shows the resulting dictionary ceiling separately.
- The dataset intentionally includes dictionary-consistent vocabulary so that keyword-level labels can be compared. This makes the detection figures optimistic relative to an unrestricted crawl of real postings.
- The engine's documented shallow guards still apply: homographs,
  morphology, implied duties, and alternative expressions outside the
  grouping pattern can produce errors. Findings are not silently fixed.
- Requirements outside the dictionary remain invisible to keyword
  metrics; the dataset records them explicitly.
